Scope: what people who care about AI, AI in education and education were actually discussing this week on X (Twitter) and LinkedIn, with Bluesky, Threads, Reddit (r/Teachers, r/Professors, r/edtech), Substack and YouTube used to corroborate that something is circulating. Look for: substantive threads that explain or argue about a new paper, product or policy; debates among teachers, professors and learning scientists; classroom stories that spread widely; announcements that appeared socially before official channels; notable posts from researchers, education leaders, edtech founders and institutions.

HOW TO SEARCH (search engines index social posts unevenly, so triangulate)
1. Start from write-ups: education newsletters, Substacks and press that describe what went viral or what educators were arguing about this week (queries such as "AI" "teachers" viral thread September 2026, "LinkedIn post" AI education this week, "on X" professors AI cheating debate). Then locate the original post.
2. LinkedIn: site:linkedin.com/posts and site:linkedin.com/pulse with topical terms and the current month and year. Results show only a post's first line, and search has no date filter, so check recency: the numeric activity id in the URL grows over time, so prefer the highest ids and open the post with WebFetch to read it and confirm the date.
3. X: search results mostly return profile pages, so search directly for posts from accounts that shape this conversation (learning scientists, education researchers, edtech founders, university teaching centres, ministries, Khan Academy, OpenAI and Anthropic education teams) with site:x.com/<handle>/status, and use write-ups or Bluesky and Threads mirrors to confirm a post exists when X itself cannot be opened.
4. Bluesky posts (bsky.app) are well indexed and dated; use them to corroborate what is circulating and to find researchers' commentary.

WHAT COUNTS AS HOT
Prefer discussions with visible reach or notable participants over engagement bait. Record who posted (name, role, handle), the platform, the date, what the post claims or argues, why it spread, and what the reaction was. Quote at most one short excerpt per post. Feature only public professional accounts, institutions and public figures; do not feature private individuals' personal posts even if they went viral. Items may be up to two weeks old if the discussion is still active this week; say so.

EVIDENCE
Every item is evidence_type social_post and is opinion or discussion, not reporting. If a post makes a factual claim, check it against a primary source and say whether it holds; if it cannot be checked, say so in the summary. Never present a claim in a post as an established fact. Section for every item: social_signal. Return 5 to 9 items, or fewer with a note if the week was quiet; do not pad with old posts.
